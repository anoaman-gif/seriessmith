## FLAGS

1. **No index levels published.** The release gives growth rates only (M/M, Y/Y, Jan-Sep Y/Y). Every index row has `value` and `unit` empty, per schema Section 5. No level was computed or inferred.
2. **Contributions are approximate.** All 11 contribution rows (`cn_cpi_contribution_*`) are hedged in the source ("about ... percentage points") and are marked "Approximate, as published". Signs come from the wording: "bringing the CPI down" is negative, "pushing the CPI up" is positive.
3. **Contribution naming is outside the schema's examples.** The same component has a contribution to the YoY change and a separate one to the MoM change in the same period. To keep one row per indicator per period, the change basis is carried in the ID and name (for example `cn_cpi_contribution_yoy_eggs`, "Contribution to Consumer Price Index Year-on-Year Change, Eggs"). This departs from the rule that change type lives in the columns; it needs a schema decision.
4. **Contributions not published for every component.** The release gives a contribution for pork's parent (meat of livestock) but not for pork, and gives no contribution for the seven non-food major categories. None were derived.
5. **Table hierarchy lost in the source text.** The saved text flattens the table, so indentation is gone. `Of which: Excluding food and energy` sits directly after `Services`; it is read as the core CPI memo line, not a subset of services (`cn_cpi_ex_food_energy`). Beef and mutton follow `Of which: Pork` and are read as further items under meat of livestock. Row names are kept flat ("Consumer Price Index, Beef") per the schema example `cn_cpi_pork`, so the reading does not change any value.
6. **Basis of the Jan-Sep column.** Prose says the headline Jan-Sep figure is an average ("on average, China's CPI increased by 0.9%"). The table column is labelled only "Jan-Sep Growth Rate Y/Y". Component rows carry the column label in `notes` rather than an assumed definition.
7. **Urban and rural rows share the ID `cn_cpi`.** Per schema Section 1 the split sits in `geography` ("China, Urban", "China, Rural"). The unique key is therefore `indicator_id` + `geography` + `period`. If the validator keys on `indicator_id` + `period` alone, it will report duplicates.
8. **Base-period rotation not carried as data.** Annotation 5 says the 2025 base-period rotation affects the monthly YoY CPI by "approximately 0.06 percentage points on average". It has no defined reference period or sign, so it is recorded here, not in `data.csv`. YoY comparisons across the January 2026 rotation are not fully like-for-like.
9. **Two "food" series.** "Consumer Price Index, Food" (`cn_cpi_food`, food vs non-food split, YoY -1.7) and "Consumer Price Index, Food, Tobacco, Liquor and Dining Out" (`cn_cpi_food_tobacco_liquor_dining_out`, YoY -0.9) are different series, both as published.

Prose and table were cross-checked figure by figure: every growth rate stated in the prose matches the table, and the prose counts (six up and one down YoY; three up, one flat, three down MoM among the other seven major categories) agree with the table. No inconsistencies found.

## Methodology and Source Note

Produced per `CLAUDE.md`: the release was read in full, every figure in the data table was parsed directly from the source text (not retyped), contribution figures were taken from the prose with each verbatim quote checked against the source, and every row is traced in `audit.md`. Nothing was computed, rounded, rescaled, annualized or seasonally adjusted.

Source methodology as stated by NBS: the CPI covers eight major categories and 268 basic categories of goods and services bought by urban and rural residents. Prices are collected under a "fixed person, fixed site, and fixed time" principle from nearly 120,000 survey sites in about 500 cities and counties across 31 provinces, including online sources. Aggregates may equal the high or low value of their components because of rounding. From January 2026 the CPI uses 2025 as its base period, with categories, items, sites and weights updated.

The source is the official English edition, so no translation was needed. The page links a Chinese (GB) edition, but its title is not in the provided material and is not recorded.

Conversion performed 2026-10-21.
